11  BHRC neuroimaging publications

Curated by: Lucas Toshio Ito - UNIFESP
lucas.toshio.ito@gmail.com

Literature reviewed through August 22, 2026

This page lists publications verified as using MRI data from the Brazilian High-Risk Cohort for Mental Health Conditions (BHRC).

11.1 Cohort and neuroimaging documentation

Authors, year and link Publication Use of BHRC data
Salum et al. (2015) High risk cohort study for psychiatric disorders in childhood: rationale, design, methods and preliminary results Original sampling and baseline multimodal MRI design, including T1, diffusion MRI, resting-state fMRI, and magnetization transfer.
Salum et al. (2025) Cohort Profile: Brazilian High-Risk Cohort for Mental Health Conditions Updated cohort waves, retention, access procedures, and multimodal overview.

11.2 BHRC structural MRI studies

Authors, year and link Publication Use of BHRC data
Axelrud et al. (2018) Polygenic Risk Score for Alzheimer’s Disease: Implications for Memory Performance and Hippocampal Volumes in Early Life Hippocampal volumes derived using MAGeT.
de Araujo et al. (2018) Effects of the brain-derived neurotrophic factor variant Val66Met on cortical structure in late childhood and early adolescence Baseline cortical thickness.
de Araujo et al. (2020) Are serum brain-derived neurotrophic factor concentrations related to brain structure and psychopathology in late childhood and early adolescence? Cortical thickness and subcortical volumes.
Axelrud et al. (2021) Neuroimaging Association Scores: reliability and validity of aggregate measures of brain structural features linked to mental disorders in youth Cortical and subcortical FreeSurfer measures used to construct and validate aggregate structural scores.
Manfro et al. (2021) Testing the Stability and Validity of an Executive Dysfunction Classification Using Task-Based Assessment in Children and Adolescents Intracranial volume and regional cortical surface area used as external validators.
Salto et al. (2021) Obsessive-Compulsive Symptoms, Polygenic Risk Score, and Thalamic Development in Children From the Brazilian High-Risk Cohort for Mental Conditions Longitudinal thalamic volume change.
Borges et al. (2023) Deviations from a typical development of the cerebellum in youth are associated with psychopathology, executive functions and educational outcomes Cerebellar volume derived using MAGeT.
Mendes et al. (2023) Generalizability of 3D CNN models for age estimation in diverse youth populations using structural MRI Whole-brain T1 images used for convolutional neural-network age estimation.
Mendes et al. (2024) GPT-based normative models of brain sMRI correlate with dimensional psychopathology Structural MRI used to train and evaluate individualized gray-matter normative models.
Grevet et al. (2024) The association between duration of breastfeeding and the trajectory of brain development from childhood to young adulthood: an 8-year longitudinal study Longitudinal global cortical thickness, surface area, and intracranial volume.
Doretto et al. (2024) Childhood maltreatment and the structural development of hippocampus across childhood and adolescence Three-wave bilateral hippocampal volumes.
Duarte et al. (2025) Enhanced neurobiological biomarker differentiation for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder through a risk-informed design Subcortical volumes evaluated alongside PGS and executive function in an ADHD risk-informed design.
Trejos-Castillo et al. (2025) Can socioeconomic status moderate the effect of a conflictive family environment on brain structure and externalizing/internalizing behavior in children and adolescents? Amygdala and hippocampal volumes.
Salto et al. (2025) Machine learning for anxiety diagnosis using structural MRI does not generalize to unseen data: results from a large developmental cohort Regional structural features used for feature selection and generalizability testing.

11.3 BHRC diffusion MRI studies

Authors, year and link Publication Use of BHRC data
Moura et al. (2016) Age-effects in white matter using associated diffusion tensor imaging and magnetization transfer ratio during late childhood and early adolescence Diffusion and magnetization-transfer measures used to characterize age-related white-matter differences.
de Moura et al. (2016) Children with Poor Reading Skills at the Word Level Show Reduced Fractional Anisotropy in White Matter Tracts of Both Hemispheres Fractional anisotropy compared between children with poorer and typical word-level reading skills.
Moura et al. (2017) Coordinated brain development: exploring the synchrony between changes in grey and white matter during childhood maturation Fractional anisotropy and cortical thickness used to study coordinated white- and gray-matter development.
van Velzen et al. (2025) Transdiagnostic alterations in white matter microstructure associated with suicidal thoughts and behaviours in the ENIGMA Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviours consortium BHRC Porto Alegre diffusion measures contributed to the multicohort analysis of suicidal thoughts and behaviors.

11.4 BHRC resting-state functional MRI studies

Authors, year and link Publication Use of BHRC data
Sato et al. (2014) Age effects on the default mode and control networks in typically developing children Resting state connectivity used to characterize age-related development of default-mode and control networks.
Sato et al. (2015) Decreased centrality of subcortical regions during the transition to adolescence: a functional connectivity study Eigenvector centrality used to examine developmental changes in cortical and subcortical network organization.
Sato et al. (2015) Temporal stability of network centrality in control and default mode networks: Specific associations with externalizing psychopathology in children and adolescents Network centrality and externalizing psychopathology.
Sato et al. (2016) Default mode network maturation and psychopathology in children and adolescents Default-mode-network maturation using fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations.
Sato et al. (2016) Connectome hubs at resting state in children and adolescents: Reproducibility and psychopathological correlation Eigenvector-centrality hubs across the two study sites.
Pan et al. (2017) Ventral Striatum Functional Connectivity as a Predictor of Adolescent Depressive Disorder in a Longitudinal Community-Based Sample Baseline ventral-striatal connectivity used to predict later depression.
Sato et al. (2018) Association between abnormal brain functional connectivity in children and psychopathology: A study based on graph theory and machine learning Graph-based resting state connectivity used to identify atypical network patterns associated with psychopathology.
Hoexter et al. (2018) Low frequency fluctuation of brain spontaneous activity and obsessive-compulsive symptoms in a large school-age sample Resting-state low-frequency activity and obsessive-compulsive symptoms.
Axelrud et al. (2019) Genetic risk for Alzheimer’s disease and functional brain connectivity in children and adolescents Alzheimer’s disease polygenic risk and resting-state connectivity.
Sato et al. (2019) Association Between Fractional Amplitude of Low-Frequency Spontaneous Fluctuation and Degree Centrality in Children and Adolescents Relation between two resting state measures, fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations and degree centrality.
Sato et al. (2019) Associations between children’s family environment, spontaneous brain oscillations, and emotional and behavioral problems Spontaneous brain activity analyzed in relation to family environment and psychopathology.
Rebello et al. (2019) Association between spontaneous activity of the default mode network hubs and leukocyte telomere length in late childhood and early adolescence Default-mode-network activity and leukocyte telomere length.
Biazoli et al. (2020) Socioeconomic status in children is associated with spontaneous activity in right superior temporal gyrus Socioeconomic status, spontaneous activity, and behavioral problems.
Sato et al. (2021) Long-term stability of the cortical volumetric profile and the functional human connectome throughout childhood and adolescence Longitudinal cortical volumes and resting state connectomes used to evaluate within-participant stability.
Rebello et al. (2022) Associations between Family Functioning and Maternal Behavior on Default Mode Network Connectivity in School-Age Children Within-network default-mode connectivity and family environment.
Dos Santos et al. (2022) Assessing atypical brain functional connectivity development: An approach based on generative adversarial networks Resting state connectivity used to model typical development and quantify individual deviations.
Sato et al. (2023) Polygenic risk score for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder and brain functional networks segregation in a community-based sample Longitudinal network segregation, ADHD polygenic risk, and symptoms.

11.5 Multicohort studies and shared resources

Authors, year and link Publication Use of BHRC data
Parker et al. (2020) Corticosteroids and Regional Variations in Thickness of the Human Cerebral Cortex across the Lifespan Cortical-thickness data contributed to a lifespan analysis.
Parker et al. (2020) Assessment of Neurobiological Mechanisms of Cortical Thinning During Childhood and Adolescence and Their Implications for Psychiatric Disorders Longitudinal cortical-thickness data contributed to a multicohort analysis.
Vidal-Pineiro et al. (2020) Cellular correlates of cortical thinning throughout the lifespan Childhood and adolescent replication dataset.
Harrewijn et al. (2021) Cortical and subcortical brain structure in generalized anxiety disorder: findings from 28 research sites in the ENIGMA-Anxiety Working Group Two BHRC scanner-site samples included in the ENIGMA-Anxiety analysis.
Brouwer et al. (2022) Genetic variants associated with longitudinal changes in brain structure across the lifespan Longitudinal imaging-genetics contribution.
Bethlehem et al. (2022) Brain charts for the human lifespan Structural images contributed to normative lifespan charts.
Groenewold et al. (2023) Volume of subcortical brain regions in social anxiety disorder: mega-analytic results from 37 samples in the ENIGMA-Anxiety Working Group BHRC subcortical measures contributed to the ENIGMA social-anxiety analysis.
Zugman et al. (2023) Country-level gender inequality is associated with structural differences in the brains of women and men BHRC structural measures contributed to the cross-country analysis of gender inequality and brain structure.
Hilbert et al. (2024) Cortical and subcortical brain alterations in specific phobia and its subtypes: a mega-analysis from the ENIGMA-Anxiety Working Group Regional structural data included in an ENIGMA-Anxiety analysis.
Kurth et al. (2024) Large-scale analysis of structural brain asymmetries during neurodevelopment: Associations with age and sex in 4265 children and adolescents BHRC cortical and subcortical measures contributed to the multicohort analysis of structural asymmetry.
Medel et al. (2025) Human development, inequality, and their associations with brain structure across 29 countries BHRC structural measures contributed to the cross-national analysis of human development and inequality.
Shafiei et al. (2025) Reproducible Brain Charts: An open data resource for mapping brain development and its associations with mental health BHRC structural and functional derivatives included in a shared developmental resource.
Bas-Hoogendam et al. (2026) Structural Brain Correlates of Childhood Inhibited Temperament: An ENIGMA-Anxiety Mega-Analysis BHRC cortical and subcortical measures contributed to the international mega-analysis.
Richier et al. (2026) Brain age prediction in generalized anxiety disorder using a convolutional neural network Structural MRI contributed to an ENIGMA-Anxiety brain-age analysis.
Townend et al. (2026) Shared and Distinct Alterations in Brain Structure of Youth With Internalizing or Externalizing Disorders: Findings From the ENIGMA Antisocial Behavior, ADHD, Major Depressive Disorder, and Anxiety Working Groups BHRC structural measures contributed to the transdiagnostic ENIGMA analysis.
Staginnus et al. (2026) Dimensional Associations Between Conduct Problems and Brain Structure Across 18 International Cohorts in ENIGMA BHRC structural measures contributed to the dimensional analysis of conduct problems.
Schuurmans et al. (2026) Consortium profile: the Methylation, Imaging and NeuroDevelopment consortium BHRC identified as a participating developmental neuroimaging and DNA-methylation cohort.
